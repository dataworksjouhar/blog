What sits underneath this page

The dashboard is the last five percent of the work. Seven source systems land as files exactly as they arrive, imperfections included, and are conformed, quality checked and modelled into a star schema before a single chart is drawn. The business is fictional, but its economics are modelled on Kuwaiti outdoor destination operators: winter peaking, evening weighted, part landlord and part operator.

Why these numbers can be trusted

Nothing is quietly fixedA gate sensor failed for two days. Rather than leaving a hole or filling it invisibly, those hours are estimated and labelled, so a manager always knows what was measured and what was inferred.
Corrections are tested against the business, not just the statisticsA second sensor double counted for a fortnight. The first correction rule also erased genuine Friday peaks, which would have understated the busiest nights of the season. It was rebuilt to compare like with like: same gate, same hour, same day type.
Tenant filings are versioned, never overwrittenTenants report sales in their own formats, up to forty days late, and sometimes revise them. Every version is kept with its submission date, so a rent dispute is settled with a record rather than an argument.
Awkward records surviveRefunds stay as negative rows instead of being netted away. Duplicate re-exports are flagged rather than deleted. Members holding both a gym and a stable contract are resolved into one person.

What this would take for a real operator

The pipeline, the model and this interface are one codebase driven by a configuration file. A different operator changes the site plan, the source formats and the metric list. It does not change the code.
